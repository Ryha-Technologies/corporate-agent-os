# Catalogue of slop patterns

What reviewers look for. "Slop" is output that is fluent and polished but generic, unverified, templated, incomplete or disconnected from the user and brand. Presence of a pattern is a signal to investigate, not an automatic failure; the question is always whether the element serves the user, the task and the brand.

## Writing and strategy
- Claims that could describe any company in the category ("all-in-one", "seamless", "empower", "unlock your potential", "revolutionise").
- Stock structures repeated across sections (three pillars, five steps) with no reason.
- Fake urgency, scarcity, awards, testimonials, logos, traction, founder struggles.
- Statistics or citations that were not retrieved; numbered references with no resolvable list.
- Market sizes as single numbers; "no competitors"; uniqueness from feature lists.
- Hedged clearance language ("should be safe", "likely available").
- Hypotheses repeated until they read as facts.

## Research
- Search-volume-as-demand; SEO pages or AI summaries as sources; circular sourcing; survivorship listicles; vendor marketing as validation.
- Simulated personas or panels presented as findings.

## Brand and naming
- Random syllables; -ly/-ify suffixes without strategic logic; one-letter variants of known marks.
- Narratives that mix verified facts and framing; invented history.
- Logos that need gradients, shadows or an explanation to work.

## UI and design
- Hero section + three feature cards + testimonial strip on every page.
- Arbitrary purple/blue gradients, glassmorphism, neon glows, floating 3D blobs, oversized rounded cards, decorative icon tiles, meaningless charts, excessive whitespace, inconsistent typography.
- Fake dashboard data, lorem ipsum, invented metrics, missing empty/error/loading states.
- Same visual style regardless of product category or audience.

## Engineering
- Code that compiles but fails the real workflow; hallucinated APIs; unsafe defaults.
- Tests claimed but not run; failing tests skipped; coverage as proof.
- Microservices, queues or AI without a requirement.
- Dependencies without version, licence or provenance.

## Process
- A creator approving its own work; "production-ready" with gates NOT_ASSESSED; role-played delegation described as parallel agents.
- Reports without CHECKS_ACTUALLY_RUN; NOT_RUN silently converted to PASS.
